## Logo System

The CareBase logo system is designed to be simple, flexible, and instantly recognizable. It consists of a primary logo and a supporting icon, each serving a distinct role across brand applications.

### Primary Logo

The primary logo is the core representation of the CareBase brand system and should be used in all standard applications.

- Use the primary logo whenever space allows
- Prioritize it across all brand touchpoints
- Maintain consistent visibility and legibility at all sizes

### Brand Icon

The icon is a simplified expression of the brand, designed for smaller or more compact uses.

- Ideal for digital applications, social media, and favicons
- May be used where the full logo is not practical
- Should support — not replace — the primary logo

## Logo Variations

### Light Backgrounds

These logo variations are designed for use on light backgrounds to ensure clarity, contrast, and consistent brand presentation.

**Primary Logo**
- Use darker, high-contrast variations of the logo
- Maintain clear visibility against the background
- Do not use low-contrast colour combinations

**Icon Only**
- Use for smaller or space-limited applications
- Ensure strong contrast at all sizes
- Maintain clarity and avoid subtle colour pairings

### Dark Backgrounds

These logo variations are optimized for dark backgrounds, maintaining visibility and a strong, consistent brand presence.

**Primary Logo**
- Use lighter or high-contrast variations of the logo
- Ensure the logo remains clearly legible
- Avoid muted or low-contrast combinations

**Icon Only**
- Suitable for compact or digital applications
- Maintain strong contrast and clear visibility
- Use only approved colour variations

## Brand Rules

### Minimum Size

To maintain legibility, the logo should never appear smaller than the minimum approved sizes shown.

- Primary logo: no smaller than **1.25" wide**
- Icon: no smaller than **0.25" wide**

### Clearspace

To ensure clarity and visibility, a minimum clear space must be maintained around the logo.

- The clear space is equal to the height of the icon
- No text, graphics, or images should enter this area

### Incorrect Usage

To maintain a consistent and recognizable brand, the logo must always be used as provided. The following should be avoided:

- Do not apply effects (e.g., shadows, glows)
- Do not alter colours outside of the palette
- Do not distort, stretch, or skew the logo
- Do not rearrange or modify logo elements

These examples are not exhaustive but represent the most common misapplications.

## Typography

CareBase's typography system is designed to be clean, modern, and highly legible. A single typeface family is used to create consistency across all applications while allowing flexibility through weight and style.

**Typeface:** Roboto (available on [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com))

| Use | Weight | Application |
|-----|--------|-------------|
| Headlines & Call-Outs | Roboto Bold | Primary headings and buttons |
| Secondary Call-Outs | Roboto Light | Supporting emphasis, secondary callouts |
| Body Copy | Roboto Regular | All body text across print and digital |

### Usage Guidelines

- Use Bold and Light sparingly to maintain visual balance and create impact
- Body copy should not be replaced with other typefaces
- Maintain consistent spacing and alignment

### Accessibility

- Ensure sufficient contrast between text and background
- Avoid overly small text sizes
- Maintain clear hierarchy between headings and body copy

> **Note:** Ensure proper licensing for all fonts. Roboto can be obtained at [fonts.google.com](https://fonts.google.com).

## Photography

Photography should feel warm, human, and dignified. All imagery communicates the core values of trust, connection, and quality care.

### Acts of Service

Moments where caregivers, staff, or family are helping in warm, human ways — hands pouring tea, tying shoelaces, passing a book, adjusting a blanket, guiding a walk, etc. Small gestures that communicate dignity and care.

### Lifestyle

Older adults enjoying life in natural, uplifting ways. Laughing with friends, gardening, sitting on a porch, cooking together, listening to music, playing cards, going for scenic walks. These demonstrate independence, community, and warmth.

### Health & Daily Care

Images that capture the everyday routines that support wellbeing — organizing medications, taking vitamins, light mobility exercises, hydration, personal grooming, or simple check-ins like blood pressure readings. These moments should feel calm, dignified, and reassuring, showing that health is cared for consistently and with intention.

### Trust & Relationships

Photos that highlight genuine bonds between caregivers, residents, and loved ones. These moments should feel warm, candid, and emotionally honest — a reassuring hand on a shoulder, shared laughter, meaningful conversation, or quiet companionship. The focus is on connection and confidence: the sense that people are seen, supported, and surrounded by those who truly care.
